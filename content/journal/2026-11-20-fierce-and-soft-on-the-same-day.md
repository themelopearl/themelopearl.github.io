I've never been only one kind of person, and for a long time I thought that was a problem.

The world seemed to want one clear version of me. The hardworking foreign student who never complains. The sweet girl who doesn't take up space. The strong one who is fine, always fine. Each version was rewarded somewhere. None of them was the whole person.

The truth is that I can push hard for what I need in the morning and sit with someone else's pain by the evening. I can be serious about my work and laugh at myself five minutes later. I can feel completely unsure and still walk into the room. I used to think these were contradictions I had to fix. Now I think they are the point.

## Armour is not the same as strength

When I scored every year of my twenties, two of the six scores told this story better than I could. One was *heart*: did I feel accepted and safe? The other was *inner strength*: could judgment bend me without breaking me?

At twenty, my inner strength was an 8 and my heart was a 3. I looked strong. Mostly, I was armoured. All that strength was being spent on keeping things out.

In 2025, the hardest year of my life, the numbers turned around. My heart was a 10, the first ten it ever scored. My inner strength was a 6. For the first time, I didn't need to pretend I was unbreakable. I was soft and strong at once.

That is when I understood the difference. Armour keeps everything out, including the people trying to help. Strength can let things in and still stand.

## On feeling like an imposter

For years I treated my softness as evidence that I didn't belong in serious rooms. If I cared too much, felt too much or asked too many questions, I worried someone would notice I was out of place.

What changed wasn't that I became harder. I stopped treating my softness as a flaw to hide. The empathy I have comes from having been stuck while others judged. That isn't a weakness I bring into the room. It's one of the most useful things I bring.

## Two hands

I don't think fierce and soft are two different people. I think they are two hands. Some days need the fist, some need the open palm, and most need both.

I'm still learning when to use which, and some days I get it wrong. But I'm done choosing only one.
