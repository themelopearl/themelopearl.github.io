Memory is a bad accountant. It keeps the highlights, rounds the hard years down to a single sentence, and quietly rewrites how things felt.

So when my twenties ended, I tried something else. I sat with each year, one at a time, and gave it six scores out of ten. Not for how it looks now, but for how it felt *then*:

- **Happiness:** did I love my life?
- **Achievement:** big, visible wins.
- **Growth:** how much I learned.
- **Direction:** did I know where I was going?
- **Heart:** did I feel accepted and safe?
- **Inner strength:** could judgment bend me without breaking me?

Eleven years. Sixty-six honest numbers. This is what they said.

## The shape of a decade

This is my happiness, year by year. It looks less like a line and more like a landscape.

{{chart:landscape}}

A plateau at the start, a peak at twenty-two, then a long valley. Some of it climbs back. And then, at thirty, the floor.

One detail surprised me. The year I reached the highest level of the Korean language exam, I gave my happiness a 3. Achievements don't always arrive with joy. Sometimes they land in the middle of your hardest stretch, and you're too tired to feel them.

## The whole decade at once

Put all six scores side by side, and the years start talking to each other.

{{chart:grid}}

One row refuses to move. Happiness rose and fell. Achievement dropped to 2 for three years. Direction faded almost to nothing. But growth stayed at 8 or 9, every single year, for ten years straight.

Whatever else was happening, I never stopped learning. That is the line that never broke.

## Two scoreboards

There are two scoreboards in every life. One counts achievement, the kind of score other people can see. The other counts growth, which almost nobody sees, including, most of the time, ourselves.

{{chart:scoreboards}}

From 2020 to 2022, the first scoreboard read 2, 2, 2. If you had asked me then, I would have said I was failing. The second read 9, 9, 9. Those were the years that built most of what I can do today.

I think about that gap a lot. It is where many people quietly give up: right in the middle of their best growth, because it doesn't look like anything yet.

## Too many roads

This is the line I didn't expect.

{{chart:roads}}

At twenty-two, my sense of direction was a 10. I knew exactly where I was going. Then, year by year, as I learned more, it fell. By twenty-nine it was a 3, while growth sat at 9.

I used to call that feeling being lost. The numbers gave it a better name. I didn't have no direction. I had too many. Every new thing I learned opened a door I hadn't known about, and every new door made the road blurrier.

If you feel less certain the more you grow, you may not be going backwards. You may simply be seeing more of the map.

## The strength that broke, and came back

At twenty, I scored my inner strength an 8. I believed it. Looking back, it was high because nothing had tested it yet.

{{chart:strength}}

By 2020 it had fallen to 2. Then, slowly, without a single moment I can point to, it came back: 3, 4, 5, 6.

The 6 is the number I'm proudest of. It's lower than the 8 I started with, but it's a different kind of number. The 8 had never been tested. The 6 had been broken and rebuilt, and it held.

## Not every number was a feeling

Some scores were plain facts. In 2019 I took the TOPIK, the Korean language exam, five times in nine months.

{{chart:topik}}

It went up every single time: 177, 194, 206, 225, 239. That is not a story about talent. It is a story about a system and a stubborn habit of showing up. Each step only had to be small enough to climb.

## Thirty

And then there is 2025, the year I turned thirty.

{{chart:bridge}}

Happiness, achievement and growth: zero. Direction: one. It was the hardest year of my life, and I'm keeping its story for another time.

But look at the last two numbers. My heart scored a ten: I felt held. And my inner strength stayed at six. When everything else went to zero, what carried me was being held, and being able to stand.

## What the numbers taught me

- **The scoreboard people can see is not the one that matters most.** My best years for growth were my worst for achievement.
- **Growth rarely feels like growth while it's happening.** Mostly, it feels like being behind.
- **Losing direction can mean you're seeing more.** Too many roads is not the same as no road.
- **Strength isn't never breaking.** It's what you rebuild, and whether it holds.

I wasn't behind. I was forming.

## Score your own years

You don't need a spreadsheet, only honesty:

1. Write down the years you want to look at.
2. Score each one from 0 to 10 for how it felt *then*: happiness, achievement, growth, direction, heart and inner strength.
3. Look for the years where the numbers disagree with each other. That is where your story is.
