Most stories about courage begin with a brave person. Mine begins with someone who had already given up.

It was November 2019. For months I had worked toward one goal: joining the team of a professor whose work I wanted to be part of. My emails went unanswered. Then an answer came, and it was the one I had feared. There was no place for me.

So I made my peace with it. I decided to go home. In my head, the story was already over.

## The strange freedom of having nothing left to lose

This is the part I didn't expect. Once I had accepted the worst outcome, the fear went quiet. There was nothing left to protect: no hope to guard, no image of myself to keep intact. The rejection had already happened. It couldn't hurt me twice.

So I called. And instead of asking for a favour, I asked a different question:

> You said there is no place for me this time. What should I prepare, so I can apply again?

I wasn't asking to be rescued. I was asking for a map.

The next day, I had an interview. Then, a yes. One phone call changed everything that came after.

## What I understand now

It would be easy to tell this as a lucky story. Luck was part of it. But the deeper lesson is about what fear does to us.

Fear is loudest when we still have something to protect. While I was hoping, I was careful. I waited, I hesitated, I didn't want to bother anyone. I told myself I was avoiding rejection. Really, I was avoiding information, because information might end the hope.

Giving up, strangely, gave me my voice back. I finally asked the question I should have asked months before.

I also learned that the question matters as much as the courage. *Please take me* puts the other person in a corner. *What should I prepare?* invites them into your plan. One asks for pity. The other asks for direction.

## If you're holding off on a call

I won't tell you it will work out. It might not. But even a no would have given me something I didn't have: a clear next step.

So if there is a message you haven't sent because you're afraid of the answer, ask yourself what you are really protecting. Sometimes it's your hope. Sometimes it's only your silence.

I still think about how close I came to never dialling. The door had been there the whole time. I just had to stop guarding it.
