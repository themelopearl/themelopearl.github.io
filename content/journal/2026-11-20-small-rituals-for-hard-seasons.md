Some seasons can't be fixed. You can't study your way out of them, work your way out of them, or wait for the right moment to leave. You can only get through them.

For me, the hardest part of those seasons was never one big moment. It was the ordinary days in between, the ones with nothing to look forward to. That is where I learned the quiet power of small rituals.

## Romanticizing is not pretending

People hear *romanticize your life* and think it means pretending everything is beautiful. For me it means the opposite. It means admitting that things are hard, and then deciding that my days still deserve care.

I can't control the season. I can control the light in my room.

My dream home has warm yellow lamps, too many plants, and old wooden furniture with stories in it. Hard seasons don't always leave room for dream homes. But I try to borrow a little of it anyway: warm light, something green, a corner that feels like mine.

## Rituals are promises you can keep

When everything else is uncertain, a ritual is a promise you can actually keep. It doesn't have to be big. It only has to be small enough to do on your worst day.

I think this is why systems helped me so much when I was learning Korean. A system meant I didn't have to feel motivated. I only had to show up for the next small step. Rituals work the same way for the heart. You don't wait to feel better before you begin. You begin, and sometimes feeling better follows.

It's also part of why I started sharing my study sessions online. On hard days, even quiet, distant company makes it easier to start.

## Layers

Looking back at my hardest years, I can't point to the day things turned around. There wasn't one. There were hundreds of small, repeated acts of care: getting up, opening the book, keeping one small promise to myself each day.

An oyster can't stop the thing that hurts it. It can only keep adding layers, one at a time, until the hurt becomes something that glows.

Rituals are my layers.
