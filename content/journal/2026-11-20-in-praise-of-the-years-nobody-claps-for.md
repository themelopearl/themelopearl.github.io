There is a kind of year that never makes it into anyone's story. Nothing is announced. Nothing is celebrated. When people ask what's new, you search for an answer and find nothing worth saying, even though you have been working harder than ever.

I had three of those years in a row. From 2020 to 2022 my life was a loop: work, home, work, home. I always felt behind. Everyone around me seemed to be getting somewhere. I was getting through the week.

Later, when I gave each year of my twenties a score, I rated my achievement in those three years a 2. Then I rated how much I had grown, and wrote down a 9, three times in a row.

That gap has changed how I think about almost everything.

## Applause measures the wrong thing

Applause is a measure of visibility, not of value. We clap at the moment something becomes visible: the graduation, the offer, the finish line. We almost never clap for the years that made it possible, because there is nothing to see. Roots don't get applause. Neither does the inside of a shell.

The problem isn't that nobody claps. The problem is that we start believing the silence. If nobody is clapping, we decide nothing is happening, and that we must be failing. I believed that for three years.

## What was actually happening

Looking back, those years were dense. I learned skills I still use every day. I learned how to keep going without a finish line in sight. I learned that I could be deeply unhappy and still do good work, and that those two things are not opposites. And I learned what it feels like to be judged by people who can only see your outside, which taught me to stop doing that to others.

None of it would have fit in a post. All of it is part of who I am now.

## If you are in one of those years

I won't promise it will all be worth it. I don't know your story. But I can tell you what I wish someone had told me:

- Keep a second scoreboard, one that counts what you learned, not only what you won.
- Be careful whose applause you are waiting for. Some of the people you're performing for aren't even watching.
- A year can be quiet and still be one of the most important years of your life.

A pearl doesn't form in the open. It forms in the dark, inside a closed shell, for years, before anyone knows it's there. Nobody claps for an oyster.

And still.
